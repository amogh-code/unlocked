What is UnLock?

The Belief

Every person has a unique game — the intersection of purpose, joy, and success that is entirely their own. When someone finds it, they become unbeatable. No one can out-compete you at your unique game.

Most of us never find it. Not because we are not capable. Because something gets in the way…

There is a word for this convergence in Japanese: ikigai. What you love. What you are good at. What the world needs. What sustains you. All converge.

Most people spend a career optimizing for one or two of these and call the result success. The unique game is what happens when none of them have to be traded off against the others.

Is UnLock for You?

Every defense mechanism you have built, every mask you wear, every belief you hold about yourself and the world — these did not appear out of nowhere. They were built for a reason. They helped you survive. They helped you navigate. There is nothing wrong with them, and nothing to be ashamed of.

But some of us arrive at a point — sometimes quietly, sometimes through a jolt — where we sense that the person we have become is not quite the person we are. That somewhere beneath the habits and the roles and the accumulated layers of who we were supposed to be, there is a truer version of us that has been waiting. And once you sense it, it becomes very hard to ignore. The signs keep coming. The feeling does not go away.

That is the beginning of the UnLock journey. Not a dramatic event. Not a breakdown. Just the moment of awareness when the distance between who you are and who you know yourself to be becomes impossible to look past.

But awareness without action stays as introspection. Action without awareness becomes motion with no direction. Achievement without either can look complete from the outside and still feel hollow from the inside.

Self-awareness:
noticing the patterns that have been running quietly, unexamined.
Self-discovery:
tracing where those patterns came from — the conditioning, the comparisons, the definitions borrowed from someone else’s life.
Self-acceptance:
integrating all of it, including the parts that do not fit the story you have been telling about yourself.
Self-care:
the daily practice that makes the first three sustainable, long after the programme, the offsite, or the conversation has ended.

A Fuller Life is an individual mindset to support yourself in the journey of moving from awareness to action to achievement.

If you feel that your truest, most unique self is somewhere inside you but feels out of reach — UnLock is for you.

How Do We UnLock?

UnLock is not therapy. It is not meditation. It is not a spiritual practice or a self-help programme. It is rooted deeply in entrepreneurship — in the belief that the people best placed to solve the world’s hardest problems are founders who build with purpose, with technology, and with the ambition to reach not hundreds of people but millions.

We have come to understand that the problems UnLock is concerned with — the conditioning, the buried unique game, the gap between who a person is and who they could be — are not problems that yield to a single event or a single intervention. They require consistent work. They require the right environment. And they cannot be solved one person at a time.

They have to be solved at scale.

That is why we back entrepreneurs. Not therapists. Not coaches. Not institutions delivering one-off workshops. We back founders who are building at the intersection of technology, innovation, and scale — who are creating the conditions for inner transformation to happen for millions of people, not just those lucky enough to find the right person at the right moment.

Technology is our lever. Scale is our measure. Purpose is our filter. The founders we work with are not just building products. They are building the infrastructure for a generation of people to find their unique game.

A Lived Experience

The Person Behind the Philosophy

Piyush Shah – Co-Founder of InMobi & Glance.

UnLock was born out of Piyush Shah’s (Co-Founder, InMobi & Glance) personal experiences of facing his limitations, doing the inner work to challenge his own conditioning, and finding his way back to his true and unique self. Hear it in his own words:

“I was drawn to entrepreneurship before I had a word for it. At nineteen, my first venture failed. Inside that failure was a state I never forgot, and that realization that I might never be the best at things everyone thought I should excel at.

Every step felt contrarian, yet being true to myself made success and joy follow.

Years of accumulation followed. Banking. InMobi. Glance. Layers added. Defence mechanisms. Comparisons. Habits that passed for leadership style. What was distinctly mine got buried under who I thought I was supposed to become.

The reckoning came in 2019, at a 360 degree feedback session with my team. I walked in expecting validation. I thought I was really good at getting the best out of people. I thought I was an authentic leader. But I heard something else. Many of them felt I wasn’t empathetic, or not listening. That night, I couldn’t sleep at all. From there started my journey of my self-awareness.

What became my lifeline was something our InMobi L&D team had built into the culture at InMobi. You can only show up as your best self at work if you can show up as your best self everywhere else. As a parent. A partner. A person.

Till then self-care had become equated to selfishness, because of conditioning. By trying to be selfless, I had ignored my own needs. It was no longer a zero sum game for me. I was far more impactful at work. Far more impactful in my relationships. Far more impactful when it came to my health.

In searching for who I really was, I found my way back to purpose. To solve for this unlock, at every pivotal stage in a person’s life. At scale.”

Piyush ShahCo-Founder, InMobi & President, Glance

When Success, Purpose, and Joy Collide

The Missions

We believe there are five pivotal moments in a person’s life where an UnLock intervention changes the trajectory. At each of these moments, the conditioning that buries the unique game is either set or reinforced, and at each of them, the right intervention can change the course of a life. Not one life. Millions of lives.

Each one deserves deep thinking and novel approaches to solve, and none of them will be solved by any single person or organisation. What they require are founders who are building with genuine purpose, using technology as their lever, thinking at the scale the problem actually demands.

Five Places. One Problem.

01·

Parenting & Early Childhood

The most consequential education in a person’s life happens before they ever enter a classroom. The conditioning that shapes who we become, what we learn to fear, what we learn to chase, what kind of person we believe we are, is set at home, in the earliest years, by the people who love us most and who have never been given the tools to do it differently.

This is not a criticism of parents. It is a recognition of a structural gap. Parents replicate what was done to them. The conditioning passes from generation to generation, not out of malice but out of the simple absence of any alternative. And by the time a child is old enough to ask who they are, the answer has already been narrowed.

The scale of this problem is almost impossible to overstate. Billions of people are living inside a version of themselves that was shaped before they had any say in the matter. And yet the infrastructure to support parents who want to do something different barely exists.

02·

Youth: School & College

India’s education system is extraordinarily good at one thing: producing young people who know how to perform. What it often fails to produce are young people who know who they are.

By the time a student reaches the end of school, most of the important decisions about their life have already been made for them — by parents, by institutions, by the weight of comparison with peers. Very few have ever been asked what they actually love. Very few have ever been given the space to find out. Their unique game — the thing they could be truly, irreducibly best at — has been buried under a mountain of expectation and narrow definitions of success.

This is not a failure of individual families, but a systemic problem, running at enormous scale, producing generation after generation of high-performing people who do not know why they are performing. The cost of this in wasted potential, in lives lived in misalignment, in the things that never get built because the people who could have built them chose safety instead, is incalculable.

03·

Young Professionals

The mid-career moment is one of the most under-served in all of human development. Millions of working professionals — intelligent, capable, accomplished by most measures — find themselves, somewhere between 28 and 40, with a quiet but persistent sense that something is not right. They are on a path. They are good at what they do. But they did not choose it, not really. It chose them, through the accumulation of decisions made under pressure, shaped by comparison and fear and the need to have an answer when someone asked what they were going to do with their life.

The tools that exist to help people at this stage are almost entirely inaccessible at scale. A good coach costs money most people do not have. A therapist is not designed for this kind of work. Most people navigate the mid-career reckoning entirely alone, and the majority never actually recalibrate. They adjust. They settle. They tell themselves a story about why the path they are on is the right one, and they keep going.

04·

Entrepreneurs

Of all the places where the unique game gets buried, the world of entrepreneurship is perhaps the most counterintuitive. These are people who, by definition, have chosen to go their own way. And yet many founders are not building from their deepest inspiration. They are building from what seemed like a good idea, from what investors were funding, from what felt like the right moment. They have the drive. They have the skill. What they are missing is the why — the purpose that is specific enough and personal enough to survive the years that separate the start from any meaningful success.

In the age of AI, starting a company has never been easier. Sustaining one — through the doubt, through the pivots, through the years when nothing is working and the story you told yourself at the beginning feels very far away — is as hard as it has ever been. Purpose is the only thing that keeps a founder going through that middle. Not motivation. Not discipline. Purpose.

The organisations that help founders find this — that create the conditions for a founder to do the inner work alongside the outer work, to build from their unique game rather than in spite of it — are among the most important institutions in the entrepreneurial ecosystem. They are also the rarest.

05·

India’s Next Chapter

India is at an inflection point unlike any in its recent history. The conditions that produce transformational companies — the talent, the ambition, the scale of the domestic market, the hunger to build things that matter — are present in a way they have not been before. And a generation of founders is emerging who are not building incrementally. They are taking on problems at civilizational scale: in medicine, in space, in energy, in infrastructure, in science.

These are founders who do not need to be told that the problem is big. They already know. What they need is backing that matches the scale of their ambition — not just capital, but conviction. The belief that building something that takes twenty years is worth building. That the problems that feel too large to solve are precisely the ones that need someone willing to spend a career on them.

The Work in the World

UnLock Foundation backs founders, institutions, and communities solving the big problems within each of the five mission spaces. The goal is a biosphere — a network where every part helps the others move the needle. Below are the organisations currently in it.

Toolo

Early Childhood & Reading

Toolo is working on one of the most under leveraged levers in early childhood: the relationship between a child and books. It is an online neighborhood children’s library that makes reading genuinely joyful, through curated delivery, personalized recommendations, and storytelling. Its franchise model means anyone across India can open a children’s library from home, taking the reach of early reading culture far beyond what any single institution could.

The vision is to build the conditions for curiosity to take root before conditioning has a chance to narrow a child’s sense of what is possible.

CodeYogi

Career Guidance & EdTech

The students CodeYogi serves are not being failed by a lack of ambition. They are being failed by a system that was never designed for them. Through Dubki, a smartphone-based business simulation, students in government schools across India get their first honest encounter with the question most schools never ask: what do you actually love? No coach required. No expensive program. Just a phone, a simulation, and a question powerful enough to change the direction of a life.

The vision is a country where a student in rural India has the same chance of finding their unique game as anyone else.

· MISSION 02 ·YOUTH: SCHOOL & COLLEGE

MU20

Entrepreneurship Education

MU20 is built on a conviction the formal education system has abandoned: that the most important things a young person needs to discover about themselves cannot be taught in a classroom. They have to be lived. MU20 partners directly with schools across India to bring students into immersive, residential experiences — summits, simulations, theatrics, Model UNs — where they are asked to lead, to build, to fail in real time, and to find out what they are actually made of.

The vision is not to supplement school. It is to give students an encounter with themselves that school was never designed to provide.

· MISSION 02 ·YOUTH: SCHOOL & COLLEGE

LaunchVerse

Talent & Opportunities Platform

Most young Indians who want to do something meaningful have no shortage of ambition. What they lack is access — to the right opportunities, the right networks, the right rooms. LaunchVerse is the platform that closes that gap, connecting student talent with real competitions, programmes, and organisations that the formal system rarely surfaces. The problem it is solving is not discovery but activation: turning the aspiration to build something into an actual first step.

The vision is a country where where you grew up stops determining how far you can go.

· MISSION 02 ·YOUTH: SCHOOL & COLLEGE

Miraqules

Deep Tech & Healthcare

Miraqules is building deep-tech blood-clotting technology that could change how the world manages one of medicine’s most urgent problems. Uncontrolled bleeding is among the leading causes of preventable death globally. The company is developing a solution that could reach patients and medics in settings where current options fail.

This is exactly what Mission 05 was created for: an Indian company, thinking globally, taking on a problem at the size it actually is.
